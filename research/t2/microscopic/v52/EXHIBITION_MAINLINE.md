# T2 — Science Exhibition mainline (v5.2, 2026-11-02)

**Primary exhibition objective:** Build and verify a high-fidelity traffic microsimulation of an actual interconnected Korean junction corridor, explicitly capturing finite turn-bay storage, vehicle-level acceleration/deceleration, OD routes, queue spillback, and controlled signals. The physical mechanism and its fidelity to observed traffic are the centerpiece; questions about policy uncertainty motivate the tests but are not the headline.

**Current executed engineering:** v5.2 Python physically locates two parallel exit pockets on the first two junctions of a three-signal corridor. Each pocket admits a destination-specific vehicle only when it reaches the bay entrance and both capacity and lane-entry gap permit. Vehicles waiting for a full pocket remain in the common through lane. The terminal through-boundary at J2 was explicitly corrected: M2 final outflow is NOT a turn exit and is not routed to P2.

**Reproducibility:** 46 unit tests pass (37 ported v4.9–v5.1 tests, 9 pocket tests). 288 exploratory matched-OD runs: 4 bay lengths [15,30,60,90]m × 3 synthetic demands × 3 signal plans × 8 seeds. 144 additional runs with 8 previously unused seeds compare 15 vs 90m. Every one of the 9 demand-policy comparisons showed decreased mean network vehicle-seconds under 90m, conditional on this synthetic model. For fixed32 signal, heldout 90m−15m = −341.6 vehicle·seconds (balanced), −345.7 (main peak), −416.4 (side peak), each with negative approximate t-interval. A 15m bay holds 2, 90m holds 13 idealized vehicles, using 4.5m physical vehicle length plus 2m stop gap.

**Scientific honesty:** No real-road GIS or measured saturation flow; no continuous lateral lane-change or taper, protected left-turn signal, pedestrians, buses, physical driver calibration, or independent SUMO output. Do NOT claim left-turn behavior is field-accurate or that 90m is a road-design recommendation. The concept that short turn bays spill over and block through vehicles is established in FHWA, Yin et al. 2011 (DOI:10.3141/2257-12) and Yang et al. 2022 (Physica A 599 127439). The exhibition contribution must be precise implementation, empirical fit, and independent verification, not alleged discovery of spillback.

**Next gate:** Select one Gyeongnam corridor, collect authorized/non-identifying turning counts, queue lengths and phase timing; represent actual turn-only geometry and conflict phases; validate independently against observed days and SUMO.
