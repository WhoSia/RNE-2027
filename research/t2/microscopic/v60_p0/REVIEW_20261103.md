Date: 20261103. Independent special-investigator review of original network v5.8 and Bezier trajectory kernel v5.9.1. **Do not promote to a physically consistent v6.0 or a road-safety model.**

## Execution evidence

- Baseline regressions 20/20, new adapter/reviewer tests 9/9, combined 29/29.
- 108 paired synthetic runs: 3 demand cohorts × (6 exploratory + 12 fresh holdout seeds) × 2 modes. 54 paired cases, simulation horizon 340s.
- Held-out difference (entry-speed advice minus baseline) in vehicle-seconds: balanced +79.96 [52.20,107.71]; main_peak +37.71 [2.82,72.60]; side_peak +38.54 [-17.21,94.29]. CIs approximate t intervals, n=12 per cohort.
- Common diagnostic threshold 2.5 m/s^2: baseline 25.2 excessive-deceleration ticks per run, vs advisor 50.9 (mean over 36 held-out runs each). Advisor directly demanded stronger than 2.5 m/s^2 one-tick deceleration in 25.0 ticks/run. **These are model-internal timestep counters, not actual crashes, collisions, or field-safety measures.**
- Historical spatial-reservation audit, 3 demand cohorts × 3 seeds: downstream-link snapshots overlapped a persistent intersection reservation for 860 of 862 eligible crossing events (2-second trajectory sampling misses two events). This is a virtual-reservation/link-coordinate semantics mismatch; a vehicle front can be downstream while its rear is in the intersection, so this is NOT proof of physical impossibility or collision.

## Implementation status

The adapter uses a Bezier curvature-based admissible turn-entry speed and an upstream braking envelope `min(v_max, sqrt(v_entry^2 + 2*b*remaining_distance))`, with a quadratic next-timestep cap. It is optional to preserve baseline engine output. **It does not yet guarantee bounded longitudinal deceleration or continuous 2D vehicle movement**; it identifies the missing state-interface requirements.

## Reviewer gates

1. One persistent physical vehicle pose and body over upstream link, internal intersection connector, and downstream link; rear-clearance and queue conservation.
2. No instantaneous speed-cap discontinuity or red-light tele-stop: upstream planning with lead-vehicle, signal and spillback constraints.
3. Swept-body space–time conflict reservations, actual surveyed lane geometry, full transition tests.
4. Matched OD/phase SUMO implementation and observed road data with temporally held-out validation.
5. Novelty claims tested against Zhao et al. 2023, Lv et al. 2024, RobustLight 2025 and canonical traffic-signal optimization baselines.

**Exhibition framing:** construct and independently validate a realistic microscopic model. **Paper framing:** measure information sufficiency, model uncertainty and stability/regret of feasible signal-policy recommendations. Do not confuse synthetic throughput changes with real-world improvement.
