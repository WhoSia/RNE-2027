# T170 — matched past, latent future and selective-risk denominator
Let X be all decision-time observable past and current state. For a fixed x, future variants u have loss J(a,u) over common finite action set A. For weights p_u>0 summing to one, the minimum conditional expected excess over future-aware individual oracles is B(x)=min_a sum_u p_u J(a,u)-sum_u p_u min_a J(a,u). It is nonnegative and zero iff all positive-weight variants have a common optimizer. Randomized X-only selection cannot lower B by linearity. A perfect prefix does not eliminate conditional future uncertainty. A support count for x estimates how frequently the training representation was seen, not whether the conditional distribution of future variants remains unchanged. I167B uses matched triples with identical prefix and label order, varying only future arrival pattern, equal retrospective weights; a finite model diagnostic rather than real traffic data. 
For fixed baseline b and candidate a(X), H={J(a)>J(b)}, C={a!=b}. H subset C, so P(H)=P(C)P(H|C) whenever P(C)>0. At zero coverage, conditional risk undefined rather than zero. Aggregate harm alone can hide high conditional harm when abstention is frequent. I167A final pooled 86/1920=4.48% versus 86/330=26.06% conditional; lateburst 58/240=24.17% versus 58/110=52.73%; alternating 21/240=8.75% versus 21/56=37.5%. This identity is elementary, not a new theorem. Future calibration must report both denominators, subgroup maxima, magnitude of excess cost, and coverage. No guarantee for arbitrary shift.
