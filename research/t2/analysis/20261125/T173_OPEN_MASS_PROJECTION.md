# T173 — open-system nonexpansion and aggregate-anchor counterexample
Let T_(u,d) be the four-cell open CTM with fixed offered entry u and downstream receiving bound d. For triangular FD and CFL v,w<=1 in normalized units, T is monotone by the same coordinatewise argument as T172, including boundary flows. For x>=m componentwise, admission A(x)<=A(m) and discharge D(x)>=D(m). Therefore sum(T(x)-T(m))=sum(x-m)+A(x)-A(m)-D(x)+D(m)<=sum(x-m). Taking m=componentwise min(x,y) gives ||T(x)-T(y)||_1<=||x-y||_1. At fixed x, changing u,d changes only entry and exit terms; min is 1-Lipschitz, hence ||T_(u,d)(x)-T_(u',d')(x)||_1<=|u-u'|+|d-d'|. Triangle inequality combines both bounds. No monotone-map novelty claim. Rejected offered arrivals are outside modeled stock. 
Given true total M, C_M={x in [0,6]^4: sum(x)=M}. Euclidean projection z=P_C(y) has (y-z) dot (x-z)<=0 for any x in C_M, hence ||z-x||_2^2<=||y-x||_2^2-||y-z||_2^2. If M is wrong, x need not belong to C_M and this argument fails. The exact constraint improves density squared error but does not guarantee improvement of nonlinear flux estimates. Hand-checkable abstract witness: true x=(1,1,3,3), noisy y=(0,2,2,2), M=8, projection z=(1/2,5/2,5/2,5/2). Mean density squared errors 1 and 3/4. For v=3/4,w=1/2,C=9/5, internal mean flux F(x)=F(y)=1, F(z)=31/24. This is an explicit counterexample, not a new general mathematical theorem.
