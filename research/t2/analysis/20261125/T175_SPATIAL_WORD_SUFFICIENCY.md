# T175 — finite-horizon spatial word sufficiency, and its cost caveat
Let a translation-invariant nearest-neighbor local map T act on a finite periodic chain of N states. An interface observable g at i depends on the adjacent pair (x_i,x_(i+1)). Induction: T^t(x)_i depends only on x_(i-t),...,x_(i+t). Therefore g(T^t(x)_i,T^t(x)_(i+1)) is a function h_t of the initial word of length 2t+2, with cyclic indexing. The spatial mean is exactly the sum over words h_t(w) n_w/N, where n_w is the directed cyclic occurrence count. A longer word histogram determines a shorter histogram by prefix marginalization. Thus length 2t+2 is sufficient for this observable and horizon; it is not generally necessary, not a claim that the histogram update closes at fixed k, and not a minimal sensor count. The restriction to homogeneous periodic maps and shift-invariant scalar observable is essential; heterogeneous maps, boundaries, or labeled local targets do not follow.
The number of distinct cyclic necklaces over 3 symbols of length N is (1/N) sum_{d|N} phi(d)*3^(N/d). For N6/8/10 this is 130/834/5934. At tested N10, k6 histogram has exactly 5934 distinct keys. Since it is rotation-invariant, it distinguishes every rotation class; k4 distinguishes 5709, k5 5916. N8 k5 and N6 k4 already distinguish every rotation class. Zero error at these resolutions is partly full reconstruction modulo rotation, not an efficient universal compressed dynamic state. Under uniform prior, entropy of k1..6 at N10=5.00146,9.10028,11.76990,12.45684,12.52607,12.53217 bits; raw state entropy 15.84963. Full rotation quotient has 12.53217 bits in this finite population. k4 saves only .07533 bits versus rotation quotient despite the remaining t2 ambiguity; practical encoding/acquisition/computation not measured.
