# T174 — conditional Jensen gap and loss-specific spatial information
For the finite abstract state X, observation Z, and internal mean flux F(X)=1/(N-1) sum_i min(v X_i,C,w(K-X_(i+1))), F is concave as sum of minima of affine functions. Hence F(E[X|Z])>=E[F(X)|Z]. Under squared flux loss the optimal Z-measurable scalar is m(Z)=E[F(X)|Z], with exact risk decomposition E[(F(X)-F(E[X|Z]))^2]=E[Var(F(X)|Z)]+E[(F(E[X|Z])-m(Z))^2]. Thus state mean plug-in has nonnegative conditional bias and excess squared error equal to the squared Jensen gap, even though the state mean minimizes state squared loss. For each interface, let affine branch L_j represent sending, capacity or receiving. Equality in Jensen holds precisely when at least one common affine branch minimizes on every positive-probability conditional state: choose j minimizing at the conditional mean, then E[L_j(X)-min_l L_l(X)|Z]=0 iff nonnegative differences vanish almost surely. For summed flux all individual gaps are nonnegative; equality iff this holds for each interface. This is a standard conditional expectation and concavity argument, not a novel general theorem. The gap is task-specific and distribution-dependent; no general improvement for frozen rule under a changed distribution, nor decision-policy performance claim.
