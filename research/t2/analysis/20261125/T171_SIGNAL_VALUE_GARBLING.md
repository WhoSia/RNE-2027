# T171 — signal value, garbling and learned-rule transfer
Let u be a finite future state, p(u) its distribution, and r(a,u)=J(a,u)-min_b J(b,u) its regret for action a. A finite signal channel K(s|u) has population optimal expected regret V(K)=sum_s min_a sum_u p(u)K(s|u)r(a,u). The minimization separates by signal output because each output can select an independent action. If T is obtained by post-processing S using a stochastic kernel G(t|s) independent of u given s, any T-based rule can be replicated as a randomized S-based rule, whose expected regret cannot be below the deterministic S-optimum. Therefore V(S)<=V(T). The uninformative channel is a garbling of every signal, and every signal can be generated from the exact future state; 0<=V(S)<=V(no information). This is the standard finite decision-theoretic information ordering, not a new general theorem. In I168A the equal-prior ideal binary signal decreases expected regret by 659.6667, but the 25%-flip channel decreases it by 50 (about 7.58% of ideal benefit). The oracle has latent group cost knowledge; these are retrospective values under fixed hypothetical channels and priors, not sensor performance. The learned rule in I168B does not attain V(K); richer or cleaner channels need not improve a fixed learned rule under shift. Robust selection in I168A is per group over a specified prior polytope; neither population transfer nor global common-prior optimality follows.
