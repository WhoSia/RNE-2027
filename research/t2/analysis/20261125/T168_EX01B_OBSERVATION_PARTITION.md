# T168 / EX01B — finite observation-partition regret and a hand-checkable demonstration
Let finite environments i have costs J_i(a) for a common finite action family A and observations z_i. Every deterministic policy using only z chooses the same a for all i in group G_z. Its empirical aggregate regret is at least
B(z)=sum_G [min_a sum_{i in G} J_i(a)-sum_{i in G} min_a J_i(a)].
The expression is attained on the finite labeled cohort by selecting a minimizing sum in each group. It is not an out-of-sample estimator or implementable predictor without learning. Every term is nonnegative. It is zero precisely when all environments in G share at least one optimal action. Refinement of a partition cannot increase B because a coarse rule remains available. For randomized observation-only policies the same expectation lower bound holds by linearity; randomization cannot beat the best deterministic action on each group. Weighted environments with positive weights have the analogous expression. Conditional cohort bounds may sum to less than pooled bound because knowing cohort is additional information. This is elementary finite decision theory, not a new general theorem.
