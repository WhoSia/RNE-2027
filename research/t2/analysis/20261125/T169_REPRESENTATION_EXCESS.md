# T169 — representation improvement versus fitted-policy excess
On a fixed finite labeled cohort, let B(Z) be the minimum aggregate regret over all deterministic Z-only rules (T168). A fitted rule pi_Z, including hierarchical fallback to nested coarser observations, remains Z-measurable. Define E(Z)=R(pi_Z)-B(Z)>=0. For nested fine W and coarse Z,
R(pi_W)-R(pi_Z) = [B(W)-B(Z)] + [E(W)-E(Z)].
The first bracket <=0, the second can have either sign. Thus fine representation improves actual fitted decisions iff its decrease in the finite-cohort floor exceeds the increase in excess. E is NOT pure statistical estimation error; it includes rule restrictions, backoff, finite-sample variation and distribution shift. B is retrospective; singleton cells trivially contribute zero, not evidence of generalization. Example I166B pooled counts vs past_movement: B11189->4979 (-6210), E2037->6481 (+4444), realized regret13226->11460 (-1766). Past_movement vs ordered_history: B4979->2451 (-2528), E6481->9130 (+2649), realized regret11460->11581 (+121). Known sample support and shifted sample support differ. This is an algebraic decomposition of the T168 quantity, not a novel universal theorem.
