# D36 — observation error in offline actuated vs fixed traffic signals

Three traffic demand levels (18/28/40 arrivals) use 4 development seeds (301/303/305/307) and disjoint 4 held-out seeds (401/403/405/407). Compare: 24s fixed signal with 10s A and 10s C greens, and queue-proxy actuated controller under (1) no error, (2) 20% per-vehicle detection miss + 1 second observation lag, (3) 50% misses + 1 second lag. A synthetic ghost count occurs with probability miss_fraction/5. Noise is generated by seed, and the same demand is shared. Bus lengths/acceleration differ from cars; synthetic bus_every=7. All policy experiments are offline simulations, not real controllers.

**Critical result:** at n=40 held-out, the mean travel time from fully completed 4 seeds is 72.9875s (fixed), 71.75s (perfect actuated), 73.35s (20% miss), 74.35s (50% miss). Therefore policy ranking **reverses** relative to fixed under these synthetic conditions, although N=4 and no actual sensor failure model calibration mean no general claim. Some noise experiments at other demand levels are non-monotonic. The simulator's queue count is a proxy, not a realistic loop detector measurement.

A completion-only mean is `None` if even one trip is censored; report unfinished count, maximum entrance queue, per-approach disparity, and a horizon-bound lower limit. No hyperparameter tuning on held-out seeds was carried out in D36, but the demand conditions were chosen following earlier exploratory results and therefore cannot be described as preregistered confirmatory research. Next stress axes: pedestrian wait, bus arrivals, independent SUMO and sensor noise from measured field detectors.
