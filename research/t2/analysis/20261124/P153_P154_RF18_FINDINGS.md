## Counterexample and boundary
Representative mask82, interval2 P1 D2, sink0, 8-period origin2, fixed S priority, same arrival tape and fixed permissions. At t8 reuse admits an S while old-space cannot; at t9 reuse can move L into pocket, earlier than old-space t10. At t10 reuse moves L into receiver (full), while old-space admits L into pocket. At t11 old-space moves S into receiver under fixed priority and reuse cannot. At t12–13 the old-space path releases two receiver vehicles and transfers the earlier L from pocket at t13; the later L waits at the shared head. Under reuse the upstream S still precedes the later L and both remain in the shared queue. At t14–15 old-space places the remaining L into and then out of pocket; reuse moves S at14, admits L at15 but cannot move newly admitted L in that tick. At t17 old-space exits all 7; reuse has one L still in pocket, receiver empty and sink service unused; that L crosses at18, exits20. Identical cumulative exits through16; at t17–19 reuse has one more outstanding vehicle, so J_reuse-J_old=3. This refutes extending P150B scalar tandem dominance to this multi-movement shared-head topology. It does not refute P150B under its stated hypotheses. It is not a calibrated field mechanism nor evidence of general prevalence. Six cases are related, not six independent mechanisms. Direct-sum extension: if each movement has independent entry, receiver, and sink resources, and each obeys P150B fixed scalar recurrences, total J is sum of individual nonincreasing J_m. This sufficient condition is a logical corollary only, not necessary or a new simulation result.
