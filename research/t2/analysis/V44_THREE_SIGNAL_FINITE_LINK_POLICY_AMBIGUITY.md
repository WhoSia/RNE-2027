# T2 v4.4 — Three active signals, finite receiving storage and policy ambiguity

Executed 2026-11-02. Locally 7/7 unittest PASS. 36 parameter worlds x 6 signal split policies = 216 runs, 50 discrete steps each. Model: three active signals, main stream passing downstream through two finite queues, and three side entry queues. Each signal shares a 5-step main/side phase schedule. No switch loss, pedestrian movement, lane change, realistic speed, multi-destination OD, calibrated field inputs or movement conflicts. It is MESOSCOPIC only; does not replace the separate IDM reference or the SUMO future check.

At current phase split=5, side vehicles receive no green. The observed corridor-level *exit count alone* therefore hides side demand. Across storage {2,4,8,16}, main arrival rate {1,2,3} veh/tick, side rate 0 vs 2 veh/tick, 12 pairs had equal baseline exit count but disjoint best split sets. Example capacity2 main arrival2: baseline exits 96 in both worlds; world A side0 optimal main-green split4; world B side2 optimal split0. In A, B's recommended split0 completes 0; in B, A's split4 completes 106. The inability to infer optimal policy from aggregate baseline exits is **deliberately constructed**; if direction-specific arrivals are observed, this particular ambiguity disappears.

**Critical limitation:** A system that never serves the side approach is pathological (not a field-feasible safe green plan), and optimization objective sums exits independent of OD trip distance; results do not establish practical policy recommendation, nor any theorem about formally constrained pressure control. Baseline signal and policy constraints must be replaced with minimum green, stage conflict, pedestrian calls and switch losses before field claims. Negative control: with observed directional arrivals the two constructed worlds are immediately distinguishable.
