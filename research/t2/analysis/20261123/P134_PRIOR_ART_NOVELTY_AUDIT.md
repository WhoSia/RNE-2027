## Actual cited work and overlap with T2

1. **Kouvelas, Lioris, Fayazi, Varaiya 2014 — Maximum Pressure Controller for Stabilizing Queues in Signalized Arterial Networks.** PDF full text discusses queuing control based on upstream and downstream link stocks. Its *pressure* formula already **normalizes link queue length by its storage capacity** `x_z/x_z,max`, subtracting turning-weighted downstream occupancy `Σβ x_w/x_w,max`, times saturation `S_z` (paper p.4, 'Max-pressure' equation). Its store-and-forward evolution `x_z(t+1)=x_z(t)+Δt[q_z(t)-s_z(t)+d_z(t)-u_z(t)]` is ordinary conservation (paper p.6). Its simulation explicitly tracks individual vehicle link entry/exit times and evaluates mean travel time (paper pp.7–8); hence P123/P125 vehicle-time identity and finite storage weighting are NOT original discoveries. The paper explicitly treats arterial offset/cycle coordination as outside its own MP controller focus (paper p.4, paragraph before stage definitions). It does NOT establish our specific sign reversal for finite red-onset fixed-cycle policies under our local refusal-second proxy; distinct research question, but novelty NOT established until other literature checked.
2. **Daganzo 1998 — Queue Spillovers in Transportation Networks with a Route Choice**, Transportation Science 32(1):3–11, DOI 10.1287/trsc.32.1.3. From accessible publisher abstract only: physical spillback past a diverge can alter route choices and produce saturation-state switching; a temporary endogenous disturbance can have persistent network consequences. Thus our '1s phase perturbation propagates into upstream congestion' must NOT be marketed as an inherently novel qualitative phenomenon. The Daganzo example involves ROUTE CHOICE, while T2 fixes all 60 IDs' route movements; the form of travel-time/proxy mismatch is not known from the abstract alone.
3. **Yin, Zhang & Wang 2011 — Modeling Delay during Heavy Traffic for Signalized Intersections with Short Left-Turn Bays**, Transportation Research Record 2257, DOI 10.3141/2257-12. University bibliographic abstract / publisher abstract state: develops left-turn delay model for finite short bays, analyses blockage/spillback affecting through and left streams, and validates with VISSIM vs HCM. Thus 'short left-turn pocket can obstruct straight traffic and increase delay' is established prior art, not our novelty. Full text unavailable on this connection; no equation-level novelty comparison completed.
4. **Varaiya 2013 — Max pressure control of a network of signalized intersections**, Transportation Research Part C 36, 177–195, journal abstract only. Queue stability vs local priority service already studied and priority-based local control can destabilize networks. Thus 'priority arbitration affects network congestion' is not inherently novel. Does not certify our precise per-second refusal proxy mechanism.

## Methodological overlap that must be declared
- P125 `J_a-J_b=Σ_i(exit_a,i-exit_b,i)` when arrivals match and all vehicles clear is an **immediate corollary of conservation**; paper 2014 already uses stock evolution and individual times, so no new theorem here.
- P129–P131 model forks change network geometry, link-specific storage and downstream exit clock/tie arbitration. They are **structural sensitivity experiments**, not empirical evidence of actual physically separate L/S receivers.
- P132 shows 2 of 3 priority-induced strict-paradox label reversals have **identical network cost contrast**; P133 shows alternative local-denial definitions yield different labels (seconds flips 3, attempts flips 2, unique-denied IDs flips 0 over 16 shared tapes). This could be an interesting **measurement validity / surrogate-objective robustness** study, but remains a *specific operational counterexample* rather than proven novel literature-level insight.
- The requirement for two objective variables (local blocked seconds and full network vehicle-seconds) and the strict rule `Δdenied<0,ΔJ>0` is a user-defined comparison. Never call the mere opposite signs a previously unknown paradox without comparable literature search.
