## Hard-budget frontier and stronger calendar comparator

16 curves × six caps (0,1,2,3,4,8) = 96 conditions × four policies. No credit price; total-stock and total-stock+4×stage-max. Exact adaptive optimum nonincreasing along every cap curve. In all eight total-stock curves, best restricted policy equals adaptive at every cap. In broad S3 L1 stage-max witness, cap4 gives 2523243/32768 versus cap0 2535439/32768; cap8 630275/8192. Equal caps do not imply equal actual expenditure. The initial combined native execution timed out after completing certificates but before writing budget JSON; rerun separately with model and planner caches shared across caps/classes and explicit remaining budget in each DP call. No hypothesis change.

Calendar challenge: 20 feasible deterministic calendars (16 head subsets, four single full stages), exact control for each, skip redundant scheduled measurements, no reallocating savings to unscheduled measurements. Sixteen class/law/objective contexts ×20 =320 evaluations. Combined class-informed comparator is min(previous restricted, optimal calendar). Positive residual gain in 3/16; broad q=(2,2) peak-weight objective 6249/32768 (0.247046%), broad q=(2,0)/(0,2) 16423/49152 (0.625846% each). All remaining 13 are zero. Do not claim meaningful traffic cost reduction generally. Nominal calendar feasibility is conservative, not full enumeration of all contingent schedules or random policies. These results support further work on when sensing is valuable and how to certify model-based decisions, rather than a large improvement headline.
